# Commercial Opportunity Scorecard

Use this scorecard to reject weak ideas early. A high score is a prioritization signal, not a guarantee of revenue.

Rate each category from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong), attach evidence, and identify the next validation step.

| Category | What to verify | Score |
|---|---|---:|
| Pain severity | Is the problem frequent, expensive, and urgent? | /5 |
| Buyer clarity | Can we name the role and budget owner? | /5 |
| Willingness to pay | Has a buyer discussed a real budget or paid pilot? | /5 |
| Reachability | Can we reach 10-20 relevant buyers affordably? | /5 |
| Delivery feasibility | Can we deliver a useful result with current skills and resources? | /5 |
| Differentiation | Why would a buyer choose this over existing tools? | /5 |
| Unit economics | Is gross margin plausible after hosting, model, support, and payment costs? | /5 |
| Risk and compliance | Can we operate legally and protect customer data? | /5 |

## Evidence required before building

- At least 5 discovery conversations with target users.
- A written problem statement in the buyer's own language.
- A competitor and substitute review.
- A demo scope that can be delivered in 1-2 weeks.
- A cost model with conservative, base, and optimistic scenarios.
- A concrete paid-pilot ask.

## Stop conditions

Pause or change direction if users do not recognize the problem, no buyer owns the budget, acquisition costs are implausible, required data cannot be handled safely, or the product depends on unsupported claims.

## Revenue discipline

Separate signed revenue, collected cash, recurring revenue, pipeline, and speculative forecasts. Never report pipeline as income. Include refunds, taxes, payment fees, support, infrastructure, and model inference in margin calculations.
